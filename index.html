Lunar Landing Site Navigator

Compare two lunar landing sites: Sun and Earth positions, solar power and direct-to-Earth communications.

Why is the south pole so hard? The Moon’s spin axis is almost vertical, so the Sun skims the horizon (0 to 3° elevation), shadows are very long, and some craters never see the Sun. Earth wobbles with libration: near the pole it rises and sets below the horizon, so direct communication works only in windows. Shifting the date by a few days can change everything.
Limitations: flat horizon (no LOLA terrain); without the Horizons file, the model is simplified and approximate (demo only).
